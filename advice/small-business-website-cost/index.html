How much does a small business website cost?

A one-page site and a five-page site are different jobs. The real question is what your business needs the website to do.

If you're pricing your first business website, you'll see offers that are difficult to compare. One company includes hosting. Another doesn't. Someone promises five pages but expects you to supply every word and photograph. The lowest number isn't always the cheapest option once the work starts.

Start with the job, not the page count

A plumber who mainly needs calls and quote requests may do perfectly well with one clear page. A business offering repairs, installations and commercial contracts may need separate service pages. More pages can be useful, but only when they help customers find something.

At SZARA, a one-page Starter website begins at $999. A business website of up to five pages starts at $1,799. Those are starting prices in US dollars, not universal estimates for every project. We agree on the pages, content and features before giving a final quote.

Ask what the price actually covers

  • Content: Are you writing your own service descriptions, or is writing included?
  • Photos and branding: Will you supply the logo, pictures and business details?
  • Contact: Is a working form included? Who receives the messages?
  • Domains and hosting: Who pays the ongoing bills, and whose account holds the domain?
  • Changes: How many review rounds are included before extra charges apply?
  • After launch: Can you make changes yourself? Who helps when something stops working?

Booking systems, online stores, membership features and complex integrations are a different level of work. They're worth discussing early instead of discovering the extra cost halfway through.

And the monthly cost?

A website can have ongoing costs even when the design is a one-time purchase: the domain, hosting, software subscriptions and any maintenance you choose. SZARA's optional Care plans begin at $79 per month. Hosting and domain fees are quoted separately unless your proposal says otherwise. Not every business needs a monthly maintenance agreement.

My advice: Start with the smallest website that clearly explains your service and makes it easy for someone to contact you. Add pages when there's a good reason, not just to make the package look bigger.
