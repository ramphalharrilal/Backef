What are you paying for with website maintenance?

A maintenance plan should tell you exactly what's being checked, what changes are included and what happens when something needs extra work.

“Maintenance included” sounds reassuring, but it isn't a complete description. A simple brochure site, an online shop and a membership platform have very different ongoing needs. Before signing up, ask what the provider will actually do each month.

Website checks and website edits are different

Checks may cover uptime, links, forms, backups where supported, and security or platform updates. Edits mean changing the information people see: a price, photograph, service description or promotion.

Some plans cover checks only. Others include a set amount of editing time. If your business changes its offers every week, an edit allowance may matter much more than a long list of technical buzzwords.

What if you already have a website?

Send the link first. We need to see what platform it uses, how it's hosted, and whether we can safely take responsibility for it. We don't quote existing-site support without that check.

Do you really need a monthly plan?

Not always. If you rarely change a simple site and have someone who can handle the occasional issue, paying for individual updates might make more sense. A monthly arrangement becomes useful when your information changes regularly or you want one person responsible for routine checks and updates.

The question to ask: “If my contact form stops sending messages next week, what will you check, how soon will you respond, and is that work included?” The answer tells you more than the word “maintenance.”

Maintenance reduces avoidable problems. It cannot promise a website will never go down, get attacked or need an unexpected repair. A provider who says otherwise is promising too much.
