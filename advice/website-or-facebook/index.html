Do you need a website if you already have Facebook?

You don't need to abandon Facebook to have a website. The two can do different things, and for some businesses Facebook alone is fine for now.

Some business owners get nearly all their enquiries through Facebook or WhatsApp. If that's working, I'm not going to tell you to stop. Social media is good at putting fresh pictures, announcements and customer conversations in front of people.

But think about someone who doesn't follow your page. They want your opening hours, services, service area and a way to request a quote. Can they find those answers quickly without scrolling through last month's posts?

What Facebook already does well

  • Sharing recent work, photographs and updates.
  • Answering messages and starting conversations.
  • Connecting with a local community you've already built.
  • Testing whether a new service attracts interest before investing in a full site.

If you're starting a side business and still figuring out demand, that may be enough. Put your money into doing good work and taking clear photographs of it first.

Where a website helps

A website gives customers one stable place to find the essentials. It can have a clear page for each service, a short explanation of where you work and an enquiry form that sends the details you actually need to prepare a quote.

You're also less dependent on the layout and visibility rules of a social platform. You still need to maintain your website, of course, but the content and navigation aren't buried between unrelated posts.

For a community organization, that matters even more. A resident looking for a form or meeting information shouldn't need to search through social posts. That's part of why we've worked on clear information pages for Kenswick Meadows Community Association.

You might only need one page

A useful first website can be very simple: what you do, where you work, a few genuine examples, and a way to call or email. You can keep using Facebook for photos, updates and conversation, with a link pointing people back to the site.

Before you pay: Ask someone who hasn't seen your business before to find your phone number, services and location using only your Facebook page. If they struggle, that's a clue that a clearer home for the information might help.

What I'd recommend

Keep the audience you've built on Facebook. Add a website when customers repeatedly ask the same questions, you offer several services, or you want a more organized way to collect enquiries. A small business website doesn't have to replace anything that's already working.
