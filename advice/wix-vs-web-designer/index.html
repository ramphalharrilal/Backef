Should you use Wix or hire a web designer?

By SZARA · Design and technology resources

If you have the time and want to make every change yourself, a website builder can be a reasonable choice. Hiring a designer makes more sense when the work is slowing your business down.

Quick answer

A website builder can suit a simple site if you have time to manage it. A designer can help when the structure, content or integrations need dedicated work.

Explore website design

Website builders have made it possible to launch a decent-looking site without paying someone to write code. That's a good thing. You shouldn't have to hire a developer just to publish your opening hours and phone number.

Choose a builder if you like working on your own site

Wix, Squarespace and similar platforms offer layouts, hosting arrangements and editing tools in one place. They can work well for a straightforward business page, portfolio or appointment-based service, depending on the features you need.

The tradeoff is your time. You're still choosing layouts, preparing images, writing service descriptions, checking the mobile version and making sure contact enquiries go somewhere. Those jobs don't disappear because a template is included.

Hire someone when the decisions become the hard part

A designer can help decide what belongs on the homepage, which services need their own pages and what a visitor should do next. That's useful when you have several services, a website that no longer reflects the business or a launch date you can't keep pushing back.

You should still be involved. The designer doesn't know your customers, policies or pricing better than you do. You need to approve the actual information and understand what's included in the quote.

Don't choose on the first-year price alone

  • With a builder: Check the subscription, domain, paid features and whether you can use your own domain on the plan.
  • With a designer: Ask what you own, where the site will be hosted, what updates cost and how you can move providers later.
  • With either option: Check the contact form, mobile layout, basic search visibility and whether you're comfortable making changes.

There's no rule that a builder website is bad for SEO, or that a custom-coded site automatically ranks well. Clear content, performance, navigation and the site's credibility still matter.

Our suggestion

If you can build a simple site this weekend and are happy maintaining it, try that first. If the site has to explain a complicated service, serve different customer groups or look professional without becoming another job on your schedule, get a quote.

Keep control of your business: Whatever you choose, make sure you know who owns the domain, who can access the site, and what it would take to move it.
