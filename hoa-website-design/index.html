Discuss a website

A useful website for the people who live there

Residents should not have to search through old emails or social media posts to find an association form or the right contact. A clear website gives them a familiar place to return to.

For a board, the website also needs to be practical to maintain. Notices change, documents are replaced, and the people responsible for keeping information current may change over time. The site should make those ordinary updates manageable.

What belongs on an association website?

The right structure depends on how your community operates. We can discuss a straightforward public site with:

  • Association notices and community news, with clear dates.
  • Governing documents, downloadable forms and resident resources.
  • Board or management contact information and a clear route for enquiries.
  • Payment information or a link to an approved payment provider, where applicable.
  • A layout that works on phones as well as desktop screens.

Private resident accounts, payment integrations, membership directories and protected records require separate planning. An association should decide what can be public before documents are published.

Before we propose a design

It helps to know which documents must stay available, who approves website updates, whether the community needs online payments, and which parts of the current site are still working. If the association has an existing domain or an older site, we can review the transition requirements together.

We agree what will be delivered before starting. Website ownership, hosting, maintenance, access and any third-party services are documented in the proposal rather than left until launch.
