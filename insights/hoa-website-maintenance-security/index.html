Website Maintenance and Security for HOA Communities

A practical website maintenance guide for HOA boards, covering resident information, account ownership, forms and continuity.

An HOA website is a practical resource for residents, not just a brochure. It may contain meeting notices, governing documents, community contacts and links to services. When information is outdated or a form stops working, residents can lose confidence in the site.

Make ownership clear

Keep a board-approved record of the domain registrar, hosting provider, website administrators and recovery contacts. Give authorized people individual accounts, enable multifactor authentication where available, and remove access when roles change. Do not share passwords in meeting minutes or group emails.

Keep information and forms current

Assign responsibility for meeting dates, documents and contact details. Test enquiry forms with dummy information and make sure the right people receive the messages. Review whether documents are intended for the public or only for residents before publishing them.

Plan for incidents and transitions

Agree who can authorize emergency changes, where backups are stored and how the website can be restored. A maintenance arrangement should make responsibilities clear, including who handles hosting and software updates. No maintenance plan can guarantee that a security incident will never happen.
