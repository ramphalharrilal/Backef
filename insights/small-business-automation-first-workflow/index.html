Before Buying AI Software, Fix One Repetitive Business Task

A practical guide to selecting a small business workflow for automation without unnecessary subscriptions or risky changes.

Automation is most useful when it solves a clear problem. If your business is considering artificial intelligence or a new software subscription, start with one task your team already repeats.

Choose a small, predictable task

Examples include transferring website enquiries to a shared tracker, sending an acknowledgement after a booking request or notifying a team member when an inventory item reaches a defined threshold. Avoid starting with decisions involving sensitive information, payments or legal obligations.

Map the current process

Write down what starts the task, the information it requires, the steps a person performs and what a successful outcome looks like. Note which steps are repeated and where errors occur. Ask the people who actually perform the work to review your map.

Check the tools you already pay for

Your email system, spreadsheet, website platform or customer management software may have built-in rules, notifications or integrations. A simple rule can be more reliable and easier to audit than an AI model. Use AI when the task genuinely benefits from interpretation, such as categorizing unstructured enquiries, and retain human review where mistakes matter.

Design for exceptions

What happens if a required field is missing, an integration fails or the same enquiry arrives twice? Build in error alerts, a clear owner and a manual fallback. Limit permissions to what the automation needs and avoid sharing customer data with tools that are not approved for it.

Measure before expanding

Test with non-sensitive sample data. Compare the time and error rate before and after the change, and check that the workflow still meets customer expectations. If the benefit is real, then consider extending it.

You don't need to automate everything at once. Talk to SZARA about practical business systems and begin with a defined problem.
