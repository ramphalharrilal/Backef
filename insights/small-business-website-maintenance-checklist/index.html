What Should You Actually Check on Your Business Website Every Month?

A realistic monthly maintenance checklist covering backups, updates, broken links, customer enquiries and business information.

When a website launches, the business usually moves on to serving customers. The site, however, still depends on software, accounts, integrations and information that can change over time.

Check what customers see first

On a phone and desktop, review your homepage, service pages and contact information. Try important navigation links, and make sure your prices, hours and service area have not become outdated. Submit a test enquiry and confirm that it reaches the right inbox.

Keep software and access under control

For a content management system such as WordPress, review available core, plugin and theme updates. Take an appropriate backup and test significant changes before deploying them. Address urgent security updates promptly rather than waiting for the monthly review. Remove unused extensions when safe, and review administrator accounts for people who no longer need access.

Know that you can recover

Identify what your host or backup service actually protects. A website database and media files may need separate coverage. Verify that backups are recent, protected and retrievable, and periodically test restoration in a safe environment. Check domain and hosting renewal dates so they do not surprise you.

Look at performance and errors

Check several key pages on a typical mobile connection. Look for missing images, broken layouts, certificate warnings and unexpected redirects. Review analytics or Search Console if available, but remember that a change in traffic alone does not establish a technical problem.

Decide who owns the checklist

A simple monthly record with a date, person responsible, findings and next action is often enough. The frequency should reflect the complexity and importance of the website; an active online store may need more frequent monitoring.
