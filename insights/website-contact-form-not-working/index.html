Is Your Website Quiet, or Are Customer Messages Getting Lost?

By SZARA · Design and technology resources

Five practical checks to find missed website enquiries, broken forms and booking links before they cost you customers.

Quick answer

Test the form yourself, including submission and delivery to the correct inbox. A focused Website Rescue repair starts at $199, subject to scope.

Explore Website Rescue

A quiet inbox can mean business is slow. It can also mean that people are trying to reach you and their messages never arrive. Your website may look perfectly normal while a form, email notification or booking integration has stopped working.

Start with a real customer journey

Open your website on a phone using a private browser window. Find the contact page without using a saved link. Submit an unmistakable test enquiry using an email address outside your business domain. Note whether the page confirms that the request was sent, and check whether the correct team member receives it.

Check every route to your business

Try the phone link, booking button and any alternate contact form. If your site uses a separate scheduling platform, follow the journey as far as the confirmation screen without placing a real paid order. Confirm that opening hours, the service area and contact information still match the business.

A success message is not proof of delivery

Check the form submission log if your platform stores one, along with spam folders and email delivery logs. A website can show a success message even when its email notification fails. If the form uses a third-party service, verify that the integration remains authorized and that the account is active. Avoid submitting real customer information in test requests.

Keep a record and repeat the test

Write down which form you tested, the expected recipient, the time and the actual outcome. Repeat after a website update or email configuration change. If the problem is intermittent, the timestamps help a technician isolate the failure.

You do not necessarily need a new website. Sometimes a small, targeted repair is the right answer.
