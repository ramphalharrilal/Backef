Someone Else Could Be Watching Your Security Cameras. Would You Know?

Your cameras may be secure on Wi-Fi but accessible through an old shared app login. Learn how to check accounts, permissions, updates and privacy settings.

Home cameras are easier to install than ever. A few minutes with a smartphone can give you live video, motion alerts, recordings and the ability to see your front door from another country. That convenience creates a question many homeowners never ask after installation: who else has permission to view the cameras?

A modern camera is an internet-connected computer, often controlled through an account with the manufacturer. Protecting its Wi-Fi connection is only one part of protecting the footage.

Think back to the day it was installed

Did you set it up yourself? Did a technician, family member or friend create the account? Was the camera paired using their phone? Has access ever been shared with a relative, tenant or former household member?

None of those situations means someone is accessing your footage improperly. But if you do not know which account owns the camera or who has shared access, you cannot easily verify that the permissions are appropriate.

What a camera account may allow

Depending on the product and account privileges, authorized users may be able to watch live streams, review recordings, hear microphone audio, speak through a built-in speaker, change alerts, delete footage or invite others. Administrative access is often more powerful than viewing-only access.

Some systems keep recordings locally. Others send them to a cloud service. Several offer both. Understanding which arrangement you use helps you make informed privacy choices.

Why a strong Wi-Fi password may not protect the video

Many cameras permit remote viewing through the manufacturer's account. A criminal who obtains usable account credentials may not need to join your home Wi-Fi. Reused passwords are particularly risky because credentials exposed through one unrelated service can be tried against another; that technique is called credential stuffing.

A unique password and multifactor authentication help. Where supported, passkeys and phishing-resistant sign-in options can offer stronger protection. Keep the managing smartphone secure as well.

Seven checks for your home cameras

  1. Confirm account ownership. The primary email and recovery details should belong to the authorized owner, not an installer by default.
  2. Inspect shared access. Review family members, guests and invited users, and remove access that is no longer needed.
  3. Turn on extra sign-in protection. Enable multifactor authentication or a passkey if the provider supports it.
  4. Update the camera and app. Enable trusted automatic updates and check that the model still receives firmware support.
  5. Review remote access. If you never view cameras away from home, consider whether remote access is necessary.
  6. Minimize recording. Think carefully before enabling microphones, continuous recording or cloud retention, especially in private rooms.
  7. Consider network isolation. A separate, properly isolated smart-device network reduces unnecessary access to your main devices, though it cannot prevent misuse of a compromised cloud account.

If you suspect unwanted access

From a trusted device, secure your account, change any exposed passwords, end unfamiliar sessions where supported, and review users and recent activity. If you suspect a device-level compromise, follow the manufacturer's recovery steps. A factory reset might not remove access to a compromised cloud account.

Security should protect privacy too

A camera may reveal household routines, visitors, conversations or the times children are home. Camera placement, audio settings and who can view recordings are privacy decisions, not just technical settings. Consider applicable local laws when recording people or areas beyond your property.

The lesson: a camera can have excellent picture quality and still be poorly managed. Knowing who can view it is part of knowing whether it truly protects your home.
